After-hours answering for contractors

SHORT ANSWER

Contractors cover after-hours calls five ways: a live answering service, an AI receptionist, missed-call text back, forwarding to an on-call phone, or voicemail. Published plans from live services like Ruby, Abby Connect and Smith.ai start at $164 to $300 a month as of October 2026. Emergency trades like HVAC and plumbing need a way for true emergencies to reach a tech tonight, while scheduled trades can usually get by with a fast text back and a call first thing in the morning.

By Matt, Dialbridge. Updated October 26, 2026.

The five ways to cover calls after hours

Every contractor gets calls at night and on weekends. An AC dies at 9 PM in July. A pipe bursts on a Sunday. Someone just wants a quote and finally has time after dinner. There are five common ways to handle those calls. None is right for everyone, and the best setups mix two or three.

Live answering service

A real person answers in your company's name, follows your script, and sends you the message or transfers the call. The good part is the human. Someone with a flooded basement wants to hear a calm person, not a menu. The weak part is that the agent is not a plumber or an HVAC tech. They can't tell a bad compressor from a tripped breaker, they can't quote, and on cheaper setups they mostly take messages. Most services also bill by the minute or by the call, so a cold snap that doubles your call volume doubles your bill.

AI receptionist

Software answers, asks your questions, and can text you a summary, transfer the call or put a job on your calendar. It picks up on the first ring every time and costs less per call than a person. It is also the newest option and the easiest to get wrong. Some callers hang up as soon as they hear it is not a person. If nobody tests it with real calls, it can get an address wrong or book a job you don't do. For emergency trades, it is only as good as its rule for when to wake up a human.

Missed-call text back

When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text within seconds: "Sorry we missed you, this is Rivera Plumbing. What's going on?" It is cheap and it works well for calls that can wait, like quotes and maintenance. It does not handle a real emergency by itself. We explain the setup in missed-call text back for contractors.

Forward to the owner's cell or an on-call tech

The phone rings through to whoever is on call. For a true emergency nothing beats it, because the person answering can actually diagnose the problem and roll a truck. The cost is people. One owner taking every night call burns out, and a rotation means paying techs to be on call. Every after-hours call reaches that person too, including the guy price shopping water heaters at 11 PM.

Voicemail

Free, and fine for a business that truly does not take after-hours work. Just know what it does. A caller with no heat in January is not going to leave a message and wait until morning. They call the next company in the search results.

Emergency trades vs scheduled trades

The right answer depends mostly on one question: does your trade get calls that can't wait until morning?

Emergency trades: plan for real night calls

  • Plumbing: burst pipes, sewage backups, leaking water heaters.
  • HVAC: no heat when it is freezing, no cooling in a heat wave.
  • Garage doors: a door stuck open or a car trapped inside.
  • Electrical: lost power to part of the house.
  • You need a person or a tech reachable tonight, plus a rule for who gets woken up.

Scheduled trades: don't overpay for nights

  • Cleaning, landscaping, junk removal, most pest control: rarely urgent at 10 PM.
  • Roofing: mostly quotes, except active leaks after a storm.
  • Callers want a quote or a time slot. A text back tonight and a call at 8 AM covers most of it.
  • A per-minute answering service mostly pays someone to take messages you could have gotten by text.

Emergency trade? Don't go cheapest, because voicemail and text back alone will lose the calls that matter most. Scheduled trade? Don't go expensive. Just make sure the morning call actually happens.

Sort emergencies from calls that can wait

Whatever answers your phone at night, a person or software, it needs the same short list of questions. Write it down before you sign up for anything. Without it, either everything wakes up your tech or nothing does.

Two rules make this work. First, the on-call tech decides what counts as an emergency for edge cases, not the answering agent or the software. Second, tell the caller the price of an after-hours visit before you roll a truck. Some "emergencies" become "Tuesday is fine" once people hear the after-hours rate.

Who answers when: the full setup

After-hours answering is one piece. The calls only turn into jobs if the next steps happen too. Here is how a full day of coverage looks when it is set up well:

  1. Cover the after-hours line. Pick the option from the table that fits your trade and budget. For a scheduled trade that may just be missed-call text back.
  2. Text back every call that didn't reach a person. Even with an answering service, some callers hang up during the greeting. A text within a minute keeps them from calling someone else.
  3. Let true emergencies ring through. Give the service or the software one rule and one number: the on-call phone. Test it at night before you trust it.
  4. Call back the overnight list first thing. Every message, voicemail and text reply gets a call before the first job of the day. This is where most setups fall apart, because nobody owns the list.
  5. Follow up on quotes that go quiet. Night callers often call two or three companies. Following up keeps you in the running. Here is how to follow up on quotes that go quiet.

Questions to ask before you sign up

Whichever service or software you look at, get straight answers to these first:

  • Minutes or calls? How is a minute counted, is there rounding, and what does each minute or call cost after the plan runs out?
  • Can it transfer to my on-call phone? How many numbers, and what happens if the tech doesn't pick up?
  • Can I write the emergency questions myself? You want your triage list, not a generic one.
  • How do messages reach me? Text, email, an app, your CRM. Overnight messages that sit in an inbox nobody checks are the same as voicemail.
  • Can it book, or only take messages? If it books, into which calendar, and can it see which slots are open?
  • Is there a contract or setup fee? Abby Connect, for example, says it is month to month with no setup fee. Ask everyone else.
  • Can I test it first? Call it yourself at 1 AM with a fake emergency and a fake quote request before real customers do.

Questions owners ask

How much does an after-hours answering service cost?+

As of October 2026, published live answering plans start at $164 a month for 50 minutes (Abby Connect), $250 for 50 minutes (Ruby) and $300 for 30 calls (Smith.ai). Larger plans run past $1,000 a month. AnswerConnect does not publish prices on its site.

What is the best answering service for HVAC companies?+

One that can transfer real emergencies, like no heat in freezing weather, straight to your on-call tech, and that lets you write the triage questions. Compare per-minute and per-call pricing against your own call volume, since HVAC calls spike in heat waves and cold snaps.

Do plumbers need a 24-hour answering service?+

If you take emergency work, you need some way for burst pipes and sewage backups to reach a tech at night. That can be a live service, an AI receptionist with a transfer rule, or forwarding to an on-call phone. If you only do scheduled work, a missed-call text back and a call first thing in the morning is often enough.

Is an AI receptionist better than a live answering service?+

Not across the board. AI answers every call instantly and costs less per call, but some callers hang up on it and it needs testing so it gets addresses and job types right. A person handles a panicked caller better. Many contractors use one of them plus missed-call text back and an on-call phone for emergencies.

Will Google charge me for missed calls after hours?+

For Local Services Ads, Google only charges for unanswered calls during your ad schedule and the business hours set in your Google Business Profile. If your listed hours say 24 hours, missed calls at night can be charged.

What is the cheapest way to cover calls after hours?+

Voicemail is free but loses urgent callers who will not wait. Missed-call text back is the cheapest option that still keeps callers engaged, as long as someone replies to the texts and calls everyone back in the morning.
