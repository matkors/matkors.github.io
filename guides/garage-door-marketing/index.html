Garage door marketing that books jobs

SHORT ANSWER

Most garage door work starts with a homeowner who needs it fixed today, searches Google and picks a company that looks trustworthy and is easy to book. So the marketing that pays is a full Google Business Profile with steady reviews, Local Services Ads if you can answer the phone, a website that shows price ranges and takes bookings online, and fast follow-up on every lead. Buying shared leads from Angi or Thumbtack is the costly way to fill gaps, not the plan.

By Matt, Dialbridge. Updated October 26, 2026.

Where garage door jobs come from

A garage door business has two kinds of work. Urgent repairs: a spring snaps, a cable comes off, the opener dies and the car is stuck inside. And planned jobs: a new door, a new opener, a tune-up. The urgent calls are where most owners win or lose the week, because the homeowner books the first company that looks good and is easy to reach.

That means marketing is not one thing. You have to be found, then chosen, then actually booked. Most garage door companies spend on the first step and leak at the other two.

Your Google Business Profile does most of the work

When someone types "garage door repair near me", the map results usually sit near the top, above most websites. Your Business Profile is that listing, and it is free. Google says three things decide who shows: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance, and prominence (how well-known you are, including your number of reviews). Google also says plainly that there's no way to pay for a better local ranking.

  • Category. Pick the one that matches most of your jobs. Google's own test is "this business IS a", not "this business HAS a." Fewer, accurate categories beat a long list.
  • Services and service area. List every service you want calls for: springs, openers, cables, off-track doors, new doors. Set the towns you really cover and hide your home address.
  • Photos. Your trucks, your techs, doors you installed. Real photos, not stock. Google says photos and videos are how you show customers what you offer, and they are part of what a homeowner compares when picking between three companies on a phone.
  • A Book button. Your profile can link straight to an online booking page. Here is how to add a Book button to your Google profile.

Keep the name clean. "Oak Street Garage Doors" is fine. "Oak Street Garage Door Repair 24/7 Springs Openers" is the kind of name that gets a profile suspended. If that already happened, see how to get a suspended Google Business Profile back. For the rest of the ranking work, read how contractors rank higher in Google Maps.

Local Services Ads for garage door companies

Local Services Ads are the paid spots at the very top, with the Google Verified badge. You pay per lead, not per click. "Garage door services" is one of the eligible categories in the US. Two rules hit garage door companies harder than most trades:

  • Advanced Verification. Google requires garage door services advertisers in the United States to pass Advanced Verification for Google Ads and Local Services. That includes a recorded video interview, a check of public records like state registrations and licenses, and possible re-verification every 12 months or after big changes like a move.
  • No pre-badge ads. Some trades can run ads while screening is still in progress. Google says that's not available for garage door services, so nothing shows until you are fully verified.

Then there is the billing. Google charges for answered calls, messages, voicemails, booking requests, and missed calls during your business hours where the caller waits more than 20 seconds. So a missed call is a paid lead you didn't get.

My take: run them if someone can answer the phone during the hours you set, and skip them for now if your calls go to voicemail while you are on a ladder. The full rundown is in Google Guaranteed is now Google Verified, and how they stack up against regular search ads is in Local Services Ads vs Google Ads.

Show a price range on your website

Most garage door websites say "call for a free quote" and nothing else. The homeowner with a broken spring wants to know one thing: is this $300 or $3,000? If your site won't say, the next site might.

You don't have to promise an exact price. Show a range for your common jobs, say what is included, and say the tech confirms the price before starting. Big jobs like a new door go to a free estimate. Something like this:

Prices on the page do two things. They filter out people who were never going to pay your rate, and they make the ones who stay more likely to book, because the biggest question is already answered. If you are worried a competitor will see your prices: they already know them. Homeowners don't.

Your site also needs to load fast on a phone and have a call button and a Book button on every page. We go deeper on that in contractor website design.

Let customers book online

Springs don't break on your schedule. A lot of homeowners find the problem at 7 AM on the way to work or at 9 PM when they get home, which is exactly when you are not picking up. Online booking lets them grab a time slot right then instead of leaving a voicemail and calling the next company.

Answer fast and follow up

Speed matters more in this trade than almost any other, because the customer has a stuck car and three other numbers on the screen. Here is what a normal morning looks like from the homeowner's side:

The research backs this up. A Harvard Business Review study of online sales leads (not home services, but the same idea) found that companies that tried to reach a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a real conversation with the decision maker as companies that waited even an hour longer. Only 37% of the companies it tested responded within an hour, and 23% never responded at all.

Three things fix most of this:

  • Text back every missed call within a minute, with your booking link. See how missed-call text back works.
  • Follow up on new-door quotes. These take days or weeks to decide. A quote with no follow-up usually just dies. Here is how to follow up on quotes that go quiet.
  • Set your hours honestly in Google and in your ads, so you are not paying for calls nobody can pick up.

Ask every customer for a review

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and replying to reviews shows you value the feedback. In a trade where customers pick fast, your rating and how recent your reviews are do a lot of the selling.

The trick is asking every time, right after the job, while the door works and they are happy. Text a direct review link so it is one tap. You can make one with the free Google review link generator, or print a review QR code for the tech to show on the invoice. More ideas in how contractors get more Google reviews.

The lead-buying trap

When the phone is slow, buying leads from Angi or Thumbtack is tempting. Here is how it works. On Thumbtack you are charged when you access or are matched with a lead, not when you win the job, and if the lead doesn't turn into work you don't get the money back. On Angi, the same request often goes to several pros, and you pay for the lead even if the customer hires someone else.

Bought leads can fill a slow week. They shouldn't be the plan. We compare the options in Dialbridge vs Angi and Thumbtack.

Where to start this month

If you do nothing else, do these in order:

  1. Clean up your Google Business Profile. Real name, the right category, every service, your real service area, and real photos of your work.
  2. Start asking every customer for a review with a text link, the same day as the job.
  3. Put price ranges on your website for springs, openers and repairs. New doors go to a free estimate.
  4. Add online booking to your site and your Google profile, so night and early-morning customers can book. The garage door booking widget takes about 10 minutes to set up.
  5. Text back missed calls and follow up on every open quote.
  6. Then turn on Local Services Ads, once you can answer the calls you are paying for.

Questions owners ask

What is the best way to advertise a garage door business?+

Start with a complete Google Business Profile and steady reviews, because that is free and shows in Google Maps. Then add Local Services Ads if you can answer the phone during your business hours. Make sure your website shows price ranges and lets people book online, so the people you pay to reach actually book.

How do garage door companies get more leads?+

Rank in Google Maps with a full profile and reviews, run Local Services Ads, and capture every lead you already get: answer fast, text back missed calls, let people book online at night, and follow up on quotes.

Can garage door companies run Google Local Services Ads?+

Yes. "Garage door services" is an eligible category in the US. Google requires garage door advertisers in the US to pass Advanced Verification, which includes a recorded video interview, and pre-badge ads are not available for garage door services.

Are Angi and Thumbtack leads worth it for garage door companies?+

They can fill a slow week, but you pay per lead whether or not you win the job, and the same request often goes to several pros. Your own Google profile, website and reviews keep bringing in work without paying for each lead.

Should a garage door company show prices on its website?+

Showing price ranges for common repairs, with a note that the tech confirms the price before work, answers the homeowner's biggest question and helps them book. Send big jobs like new doors to a free estimate.

Can customers book a garage door repair online?+

Yes. A booking page lets them pick the problem, see your price range and choose a time window, day or night. You can add it to your website and as the booking link on your Google Business Profile.
