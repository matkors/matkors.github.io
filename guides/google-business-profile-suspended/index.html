Google Business Profile suspended?

SHORT ANSWER

Google suspends a Business Profile when it decides the profile breaks its guidelines, and for service-area businesses the usual reasons are a visible home or virtual-office address, extra keywords in the business name, or a duplicate profile. Fix the problem first, then file one appeal in Google's Business Profile appeals tool with documents that match your profile, like a business license or utility bill. Google says appeal reviews take up to 5 business days.

By Matt, Dialbridge. Updated October 26, 2026.

What a suspension looks like

You usually find out one of two ways. Google emails you, or the calls from Maps stop and you go looking. When you sign in, your profile shows a notice that it is suspended or disabled, sometimes with the policy Google thinks you broke.

Google uses a few different words for this, and they mean different things:

  • Suspended or disabled profile. Google says it may "suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow our guidelines." This is the one most owners hit.
  • Restricted account. The problem is your Google account, not one profile. Every profile that account manages gets suspended, and you can't create or claim new ones until the account is fixed.
  • Removed profile. The public can't see it and you can't manage it. You can still ask for it to be reinstated.

Owners and agencies also talk about "soft" and "hard" suspensions. Those are not Google's words, but they are useful. A soft suspension means the profile still shows up but you can't manage it. A hard suspension means it is gone from Google and Maps. Here is how to tell which one you have:

Do not make a new profile. Google says not to create a new profile for the same business while one is suspended. You would start over with no reviews, and the duplicate can make things worse.

Why service-area businesses get suspended

A service-area business is one that drives to customers instead of having them come in: plumbers, roofers, cleaners, garage door techs, junk haulers. Two more causes you can't see from the profile. Google runs automatic sweeps of some categories, so a profile can go down without anything changing. And a competitor can report you. In both cases the fix is the same: make sure the profile follows every rule, then appeal.

Business names: what Google allows

The name is where most trade businesses slip. Google says your name should reflect your "real-world name" and that adding unnecessary information "could result in the suspension of your Business Profile." Some examples using a made-up company:

Rivera Plumbing

The name on the truck, the invoices and the registration.

Rivera Plumbing Emergency Plumber Edison NJ

Service words and a town added. Put those in your services and service area.

Rivera Plumbing 24/7

Hours don't go in the name. Set them in your hours.

RIVERA PLUMBING

Google doesn't allow fully capitalized words in the name.

Rivera Plumbing 732-555-0148

No phone numbers or website addresses in the name.

Rivera Plumbing, Edison's Most Trusted Plumber

A marketing tagline. Save it for your website.

?Rivera Plumbing LLC

Only if that is how you really go by, and you can prove it.

Yes, you will see competitors with keyword-stuffed names ranking above you. That doesn't make it safe. It just means they have not been caught yet.

How to appeal a suspended profile

You do this yourself, in Google's Business Profile appeals tool. It is a short form. The hard part is the prep, because Google says each decision can only be appealed once.

  1. Fix the profile first. Google says to make sure the profile follows all the guidelines before you appeal. Go through the checklist above and change what needs changing.
  2. If your account is restricted, start there. Appeal the account on the My accounts page. Profile appeals come after the account is back.
  3. Open the appeals tool and sign in with the Google account that owns the profile. Pick the profile and the decision you are appealing. The tool shows the date, the reason and the policy.
  4. Upload your evidence and explain. Keep it short and factual: what the business is, where you work from, and what you fixed.
  5. Submit and wait for the email. You can check the status in the tool at any time.

Documents Google accepts

Google lists these as evidence for an appeal:

Business registrationFrom your state
Business licenseTrade or local license
Tax certificateWith the business name
Utility billElectric, phone, water or internet

The rule that sinks most appeals: the business name and address on every document should match the profile. If your truck says "Rivera Plumbing" and your registration says "JR Home Services LLC", expect questions. Bring a document that ties the two together.

How long it takes

The tool shows one of a few statuses: Submitted, Approved, Not Approved, Can't be appealed, or Eligible for appeal. If you are approved, the profile comes back with its owners and managers. If you are not, you can ask for another review, but you need new evidence. Sending the same documents again won't change the answer.

Got 10 or more locations suspended? Answer "Yes" to the bulk question in the tool and attach a spreadsheet with each Business Profile ID plus the documents.

How to verify a Google Business Profile

Verification is how Google confirms you run the business. A new profile needs it, and Google can ask you to verify again after you change your details, like a new address. You don't get to choose the method. Google says the options are "automatically determined by Google and can't be changed." These are the ones you might see:

Phone or textA code by call or SMS. Phone menus (IVR) won't get it, so the line has to reach a person.
EmailA link sent to an address shown on screen.
Video recordingGoogle recommends it when it's offered. Details below.
Live video callWith a Google rep, only during your business hours. Not offered to everyone.
PostcardA 5-digit code by mail. Most arrive within 14 days. Codes expire after 30 days.

After you submit, Google says reviews take up to 5 business days. While a postcard is on its way, don't edit your name, address or category, or the code won't work.

Recording a verification video as a service-area business

This is where tradespeople get stuck, because there is no storefront to film. Google's rules for the video: record it live from your phone through your Business Profile, at least 30 seconds, one take with no edits. Then show three things:

1Where you work from

Street signs or landmarks near your business address. If you work from home, signs in your neighborhood are fine. Avoid empty land.

2That the business exists

Your tools, equipment, branded shirt or business cards.

3That you run it

Unlock your branded van, do a bit of the work, or show a permit, invoice or utility bill with the same business name.

Keep bank, tax and ID numbers out of the shot, and don't film other people's faces. If the video fails, Google tells you why. Tap "Review issues" and record again with everything from the first video plus what was missing.

Set up a service-area profile that stays up

Once you are back, set the profile up so it doesn't happen again. Here is the setup that keeps a trade business inside the rules:

Address

Hidden. If customers don't come to you, remove it and list only your service area.

Service area

Up to 20 cities or ZIP codes, no more than about 2 hours of driving from your base.

Name

Your real-world name. Nothing added.

Categories

The fewest that describe what you are. Google's test is "this business IS a", not "HAS a".

Phone

A local number that rings you, under your control.

Profiles

One per business. Find and remove old duplicates.

One judgment call: if your shop or yard is somewhere customers really can visit, during set hours, with a sign, you can show the address and also set a service area. Google calls that a hybrid business. If you are honest that nobody comes by, hide it. Pretending to have a storefront is a fast way back to a suspension.

Make changes slowly, too. A profile that changes its name, address and category in one sitting looks like someone took it over. Edit one thing, wait for it to go live, then do the next.

Questions owners ask

Why was my Google Business Profile suspended?+

Google suspends profiles it thinks break the Business Profile guidelines. For service-area businesses the common reasons are a visible address customers never visit, a virtual office or PO box, extra keywords in the business name, a duplicate profile, or a restricted Google account.

How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?+

Google says appeal reviews can take up to 5 business days. You get the decision by email and can check the status in the appeals tool.

Should I create a new profile if mine is suspended?+

No. Google says not to create a new profile for the same business. Fix the cause and appeal the suspended one instead.

What documents does Google accept for a reinstatement appeal?+

Google lists official business registration, a business license, tax certificates and utility bills. The business name and address on them should match your profile.

Can a service-area business show its address on Google?+

Only if customers really come to that address. Google says a service-area business should hide its address and list a service area instead, up to 20 areas within about 2 hours of driving.

How do I verify my Google Business Profile without a storefront?+

Google picks the method for you. If it asks for a video recording, it has to be at least 30 seconds, live and unedited, showing nearby street signs, your tools or equipment, and proof you run the business, like unlocking a branded van.

Can I appeal more than once?+

Each decision can be appealed once. If the appeal is not approved, you can request another review, but only with new evidence.
