Google Guaranteed is now Google Verified

SHORT ANSWER

On October 20, 2025, Google replaced the Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified badges with one badge: Google Verified. The money-back guarantee ended with it. You still get the badge the same way, by passing the free screening for Local Services Ads, and you still pay per lead, not per click.

By Matt, Dialbridge. Updated October 26, 2026.

What changed in October 2025

For years, home service companies running Local Services Ads earned a green "Google Guaranteed" check mark. If a customer booked through the ad and was unhappy with the work, Google could refund them up to a limit. That promise was the selling point of the badge.

In August 2025 Google emailed advertisers that, as of October 20, 2025, it would be "simplifying badging by launching a single, more cohesive Google Verified badge for all advertisers." The same email said: "The Money Back Guarantee associated with the Google Guarantee badge will also be discontinued."

If you were already verified, Google moved you to the new badge on its own. Nothing to reapply for, as long as your license and insurance documents stay current.

Still saying "Google Guaranteed" on your truck or website? The badge and the refund promise are gone, so take the claim down. Saying a customer is covered by a guarantee that no longer exists is the kind of thing that turns into an angry review.

What the Google Verified badge means

The badge tells a homeowner that Google checked your business before showing your ad. Depending on your trade and location, those checks can include:

  • License check. Google confirms you hold the state or local license your trade needs.
  • Insurance check. In trades that need coverage, you upload a certificate of insurance.
  • Background checks. In certain categories and areas, Google runs checks on the business, the owner and the field workers who go into homes.

It is not a quality rating and it does not move you up on its own. It is the entry ticket. You need it for your Local Services ad to run at all.

How to get Google Verified

  1. Sign up for Local Services Ads with your business name, service area and the jobs you do. Pick only services you actually take.
  2. Submit your documents. License numbers and a certificate of insurance where your trade needs them.
  3. Complete the background checks for whoever Google asks for: the business, the owner, and in some trades every technician who goes into homes. Google does not charge for these.
  4. Link your Google Business Profile so your reviews and hours show on the ad.
  5. Set a weekly budget and turn the ad on once Google approves you.

How long it takes: Google says screening takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. Some new advertisers get accelerated verification and can start sooner, but then have 14 days to finish the remaining steps or the ad stops.

If you fail: after a failed background check you wait 30 days to reapply. A second denial means a 180-day wait. Get the paperwork right the first time.

What Local Services Ads cost

There is no fee for the badge and no monthly subscription. You pay for leads. Older articles that quote a flat monthly price for Google Guaranteed are out of date.

Here is what Google counts as a lead you pay for, and what it does not:

One more rule worth knowing. If Google later decides a charged lead was invalid, low quality or a duplicate, it credits your account automatically. You can also flag a bad lead yourself in the lead's feedback survey.

The price per lead depends on your trade, your town and how hard competitors bid. You set an average weekly budget, and Google stops showing the ad once you hit the monthly maximum. Google recommends its "Maximize leads" bid setting, where it sets your bid for you. Your bid is a ceiling, so you often pay less.

Notice the missed-call rule. You pay for a missed call during business hours even though you never spoke to the customer. Every unanswered ring is money spent on a job you did not get.

How Google decides who shows first

Google says three things decide your position:

Your bid

The most you will pay for a lead. You often pay less.

Chance of a lead

How often you answer, how well your services match, and whether messages and bookings are on.

Profile quality

Rating, review count, photos and how fast you respond.

The two dark ones are where owners win without spending more:

  • Responsiveness. Google says plainly that missed calls may hurt you. Answering fast is a ranking factor, not just good manners.
  • Reviews. Your rating and review count show on the ad and feed profile quality. See how contractors get more Google reviews.
  • Contact options. Turning on messages and bookings gives customers more ways to reach you.
  • Photos and details. Google says profiles with good images may rank higher and can pay less per lead.
  • Relevance. List the services you really do and the towns you really cover.

Is Google Verified worth it?

Worth it if

  • You can answer the phone during business hours, or have someone who can.
  • Your trade gets urgent calls: plumbing, HVAC, garage doors, locksmiths, pest control.
  • You already have reviews to show on the ad.
  • You know your booking rate and average job, so you can check the math above.

Skip it for now if

  • You are on a roof all day and calls go to voicemail. You will pay for those missed calls.
  • You have no reviews yet. Start there first.
  • You are already booked out for weeks.
  • Your license or insurance is not in order for every service you list.

Local Services Ads also work well next to regular Google Ads, not instead of them. We compare the two in Local Services Ads vs Google Ads.

Google Verified vs Google Screened vs License Verified

These used to be three different badges. Google Screened went mostly to professional services like lawyers, financial planners and real estate agents. License Verified by Google showed a license check without the full screening. Since October 20, 2025, all of them show as Google Verified. People still search for the old names, so expect customers to ask about "Google Guaranteed" for a while. The honest answer is that you are Google Verified, which means Google checked your license, insurance and background where your trade requires it.

Get more out of every lead

The ad gets the phone to ring. What you do next decides whether you paid for a job or for nothing.

  1. Answer during the hours you set. Your business hours in your Google profile are the hours Google charges missed calls. Set them to hours you can actually cover.
  2. Text back every missed call right away. A quick text keeps the caller from dialing the next ad down. Here is how missed-call text back works.
  3. Follow up on quotes. Most jobs that go quiet are not lost, just forgotten. See how to follow up on quotes that go quiet.
  4. Ask every happy customer for a review. More reviews, better profile quality, better position.

Questions owners ask

Is Google Guaranteed still available?+

No. On October 20, 2025, Google replaced Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified by Google with one Google Verified badge. You get it by passing Local Services Ads screening.

Does Google still offer a money-back guarantee?+

No. Google said the money-back guarantee tied to the Google Guaranteed badge was discontinued along with the badge.

How much does Google Verified cost?+

The badge and the background checks are free. You pay per lead from Local Services Ads: answered calls, missed calls over 20 seconds during business hours, messages, voicemails and booking requests. The price per lead depends on your trade, town and competition.

How long does it take to get Google Verified?+

Google says screening takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. Some businesses get accelerated verification and must finish the remaining steps within 14 days.

Do I need Google Verified to show up in Google Maps?+

No. Your Google Business Profile shows in Maps for free. Google Verified is only for Local Services Ads, the paid spots at the top of the results.

Can I still say I am Google Guaranteed?+

You should not. The badge and its money-back guarantee no longer exist, so update your website, trucks and print materials to say Google Verified instead.
