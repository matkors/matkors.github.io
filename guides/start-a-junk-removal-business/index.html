How to start a junk removal business

SHORT ANSWER

Start with a used truck or a dump trailer, a registered business, liability and commercial auto insurance, and a call to your local transfer station to learn what dumping costs per ton. Price jobs by how much of your truck they fill, with the dump fee built into every price. Get the first jobs from people you know, realtors and property managers, and a free Google Business Profile with real reviews.

By Matt, Dialbridge. Updated October 26, 2026.

What you need on day one

Junk removal is one of the cheapest home service businesses to start. There is no trade exam to pass first. You need a way to haul, a place to dump, a strong back (or a helper with one) and a phone that someone answers.

The owners who struggle in year one usually do not struggle with the lifting. They struggle with money: a new truck bought before the first job, prices guessed without knowing the dump fee, and no insurance when a dresser goes through a drywall corner. Here is where I would put the first dollars, and where I would not.

Spend on this

  • Liability and commercial auto insurance before the first paid job.
  • A used truck or dump trailer in good mechanical shape.
  • Two dollies, straps, tarps, heavy gloves, a contractor broom and a shop vac.
  • A free Google Business Profile and a simple website where people can book.

Skip for now

  • A new truck or a new box truck. Buy used and prove the demand first.
  • A full wrap. Door magnets and a clean truck are enough at the start.
  • Paid ads before you have a few Google reviews.
  • Expensive job software. A calendar and a notes app carry you for the first months.

What it costs to start

Nobody can give you one honest number, because the truck decides almost everything and used truck prices swing a lot from one area to the next. So put in your own prices. Two of the starting numbers are real: the SBA says registering a business usually costs less than $300, and the dump fee is King County, Washington's 2026 rate for self-haulers. Before you buy any trailer, check the towing capacity in your truck's manual and compare it with the trailer's loaded weight. Wet yard waste, shingles and concrete get heavy fast. A used trailer with a sound frame, good tires and a working hydraulic pump beats a shiny one with a weak pump. Have a mechanic look at any used truck before you hand over cash.

Register the business

The SBA lays out the official steps to launch a business. For a junk removal company, they come down to this:

  1. Pick a name you can keep. Something with your town or street in it is easy to remember. Make sure no one nearby already uses it.
  2. Choose a structure. A sole proprietorship is the simplest, but the SBA notes your business and personal assets are not separate. An LLC protects owners from personal liability in most instances. For a business that carries other people's heavy furniture through their homes, I would form the LLC.
  3. Register with your state and, if you use a trade name, register that too. The SBA says most states require it and the total usually comes in under $300.
  4. Get a free EIN from the IRS. The IRS says you never have to pay a fee for one.
  5. Open a business bank account and run every dump ticket and fuel receipt through it.

Licenses, permits and dump rules

The SBA says license requirements vary by state, county and city, and that is very true here. Plan on a general business license from your city or county. Then ask the city clerk or public works office whether you need a hauler permit or a registration for each truck to haul debris for pay. Some places want one, some do not, and the person on the phone will know.

The bigger rules are about what you dump, not what you drive. Sort every load before it leaves the driveway:

  • Fridges, freezers and window air conditioners. The EPA says whoever does the final disposal must make sure the refrigerant is recovered first. If the appliance arrives already empty, that person must keep a signed statement saying who recovered it and when. Ask your scrap yard or transfer station how they handle this before you haul your first fridge.
  • Household hazardous waste. The EPA lists paints, cleaners, oils, batteries and pesticides as products that can contain hazardous ingredients. The EPA says to check with your local solid waste agency for collection days or drop-off sites. Know where yours is, or decline those items.
  • Mattresses. California, Connecticut, Oregon and Rhode Island have statewide mattress recycling programs through Bye Bye Mattress. King County, Washington lists a mattress rate of $1,090 a ton at its Cedar Hills landfill for 2026, several times its regular rate. Check your own station before you quote one.

Call the transfer station before you print a single card. Ask what they take, the price per ton, the minimum charge, their hours, and whether haulers need a commercial account. That one call sets the floor under every price you will ever quote.

Insurance

Do not do a paid job without it. The SBA describes general liability as coverage for bodily injury and property damage, which is exactly what a scraped hardwood floor or a cracked door frame is. Two more matter for junk haulers:

  • Commercial auto. The Utah Insurance Department, as one example, says that if you use your vehicle for anything beyond driving to work and personal use, you may need a commercial auto policy or an endorsement. Tell your agent you haul debris for pay and get the answer in writing.
  • Workers' compensation once you hire. The SBA lists workers' compensation, unemployment and disability insurance as required for businesses with employees, and says some states require more.

When you add a helper, know the market. The BLS puts the median pay for hand laborers and material movers at $18.38 an hour (May 2025). A good helper who shows up on time is worth paying above that.

How to price junk removal jobs

Most junk haulers price by volume: how much of the truck a job fills. Customers understand it, you can quote from a couple of photos, and it grows with your real costs. A bigger load means more dump fee, more time and more fuel.

How to set your full-load price. Start from what one full load costs you, then add profit. Your cost is the dump fee (tons times the rate), labor (hours times people times pay), fuel and the time driving to the station. Say a full load of mixed junk weighs 1.5 tons and your station charges $229.29 a ton, like King County in 2026. The dump fee alone is about $344 before you pay anyone. Price below that and you are paying to haul people's couches.

A few rules that keep you out of trouble:

  • Heavy debris gets its own price. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles and tile fill your weight limit long before they fill the box. Price them by weight or with a surcharge.
  • Add item fees for anything that costs you extra to dispose of: appliances with refrigerant, mattresses where there is a fee, tires.
  • Quote from photos, confirm on site. Text a range from the photos, then give the final price in person before you load anything.
  • Set a minimum charge so a single chair still pays for the drive.

Getting your first ten jobs

Your first jobs come from people, not ads. Paid ads before you have reviews mostly buy you clicks from people who pick the company with 80 reviews.

  1. Tell everyone you know, and ask them to tell one neighbor. Do the first jobs right and take before and after photos of every one.
  2. Visit realtors and property managers. Empty houses before a listing and tenant move-outs need clearing on a deadline. Leave a card and say you can come within a day or two.
  3. Talk to estate sale companies and storage facilities. They see leftovers every week.
  4. Post in local groups with real photos of your work and your real name.
  5. Ask every happy customer for a Google review the same day, while the empty garage still feels good.

Getting found on Google

When someone types "junk removal near me", the map results decide who gets the call. Google says those results are based on relevance, distance and prominence, that more reviews and positive ratings can help, and that there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

  • Set up your Google Business Profile. It is free. If customers do not come to your address, Google says to remove it and list the areas you serve instead. You can add up to 20 service areas, and Google suggests keeping the whole area within about 2 hours of driving from your base.
  • Get your first reviews. Make your direct review link with our free review link generator and text it after every job. Do not offer discounts for reviews. Google strictly prohibits incentives. More on this in how to get more Google reviews.

Questions owners ask

How much does it cost to start a junk removal business?+

Most of the cost is the truck or trailer, and used prices vary a lot by area. The SBA says registering a business usually costs under $300, and a free Google Business Profile costs nothing. Budget for insurance and keep a month of dump fees in the bank. Use the calculator on this page with your own prices.

Do I need a license to start a junk removal business?+

Usually you need a general business license from your city or county. Some places also require a hauler permit or a registration for each truck that hauls debris for pay. Requirements vary by state, county and city, so ask your city clerk or public works office.

How do junk removal companies charge?+

Most charge by volume: a share of a full truckload, such as a quarter or half load, with a minimum charge. Heavy debris like concrete or shingles is often priced by weight, and items that cost extra to dispose of, like appliances with refrigerant, get an item fee.

Can I start a junk removal business with a pickup truck?+

Yes. Many owners start with a pickup for single items and small loads, then add a dump trailer. Check your truck's towing capacity before you buy a trailer, and tell your insurance agent you will haul for pay.

What can a junk removal company not take to the dump?+

It depends on your county, but plan on special handling for fridges, freezers and window air conditioners (refrigerant must be recovered), household hazardous waste like paint, oils, batteries and pesticides, and sometimes mattresses and electronics. Call your transfer station for its list.

Do I need an LLC for junk removal?+

It is not required. The SBA notes a sole proprietor's business and personal assets are not separate, while an LLC protects owners from personal liability in most instances. For work inside customers' homes, an LLC plus general liability insurance is a sensible start.
