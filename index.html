Voice · Regulated

A voice agent that is not allowed to improvise

Contract voice engineering for a $150M health insurance marketplace · 2026, ongoing

Most voice AI is a booking bot for a salon. This one sits inside health insurance, where an agent that says the wrong thing about coverage is not a bad customer experience, it is a compliance problem. Everything the agent says has to hold up against AI disclosure requirements and CMS marketing rules, and it cannot improvise about plans, prices or eligibility.

I built two agents. The first is a smart IVR that works out what a caller actually needs and routes them, sized for a call volume in the tens of thousands per week. The second is an intake line for people who have just lost their health insurance along with their job, which qualifies the caller and warm transfers them to a licensed human rather than trying to solve it.

The guardrail step is the whole job. Everything else is plumbing.

What it took

I scoped both agents by sitting with non-technical executives, built the flows, presented them back, and coordinated across departments to get them shipped. The engagement has been self directed throughout: nobody handed me a spec.

Voice · Retained

An inbound receptionist that has been running for eight months

Luxury watch dealer, roughly $500K a year on Shopify · Retell and n8n

Building a voice agent is the easy part. Keeping one alive on someone's real phone line, month after month, is the part most people never get to. This one answers every inbound call, looks up orders against Shopify, handles what it can and escalates what it cannot.

The part I care about most is the loop at the end. Every week I listen to real conversations, find the places it handled something badly, and fix them. That is the difference between a demo and a system.

Outbound · Lead generation

A LinkedIn pipeline that replied at ten times benchmark

US biotech, DNA library · Apify, n8n and Clay

The client needed to reach a narrow technical audience where the usual volume approach does not work, because the list is small and the wrong message burns it. So the system spends its effort on targeting and enrichment rather than send volume.

Apify builds the raw list, n8n orchestrates, Clay enriches each prospect enough that the message can say something true and specific about them, and replies get tracked back so the targeting improves rather than repeating.
