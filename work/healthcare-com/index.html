01

The constraint set

Everything the agent says has to survive a compliance review, so the build inverts.

  • Disclose that it is AI, early enough to matter and clearly enough that a distressed caller registers it.
  • State it is not the government. Nothing may imply endorsement by Medicare, CMS or any federal agency.
  • Carry the Medicare marketing disclaimer. Third-party marketing of Medicare Advantage requires CMS-mandated wording about not offering every plan in the caller’s area, plus the pointer to 1-800-MEDICARE.
  • Never assert an outcome it was not given. No eligibility, no premium, no coverage decision, and no hedged version of any of them.

In a normal voice project the hard part is making the agent sound natural across a long tail. Here the hard part is making it refuse cleanly, stay inside a defined scope, and hand off before it guesses. Callers also cannot classify themselves: they describe a situation, not a product, so a menu routes them wrong before the agent gets a chance.

02

The decision ladder

My framework, not theirs, so I can show it in full.

  • Answer. In scope, and the answer already exists in approved copy.
  • Answer with attached disclosure. In scope, but regulation attaches specific words. The wording fires every time, not when the model judges it relevant.
  • Refuse and redirect. Out of scope but adjacent. Say it cannot answer, say who can, offer the handoff.
  • Stop and transfer. Anything touching eligibility, price or a coverage decision goes straight to a licensed human.

Most teams ship an agent that treats the last rung as an error state. It is not an error, it is the product working. Designing it as a first-class path rather than a fallback is most of the job.

03

Testing something that is not allowed to be wrong

The refusal path is the product, so it gets tested hardest.

  • Adversarial cases first. “So will my insulin be covered?” in 6 phrasings, the polite ones and the desperate ones.
  • Every transfer is an assertion. A handoff that dials into nothing is worse than no agent at all, and it only shows up if you test the receiving end rather than the intent to transfer.
  • Regression-test disclosures after any prompt change. An edit that improves the greeting can stop required wording firing 3 turns later. That is a compliance event, not a bug.
  • Tune for latency. A correct answer that arrives 2 seconds late loses the call anyway.
